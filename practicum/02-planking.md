## 1. Getting Started

### Stage 1 gate

- [ ] Hull is fair.
- [ ] Rabbet is clean.
- [ ] Stem/stern transitions are correct.
- [ ] Plank stock sorted.
- [ ] Bending method tested.

Use a long sanding block and a thin batten. Do not sand hollows into the bulkhead profiles.

## 2. Planking Battens and Belts

Bluenose is divided into four planking belts and **does not require stealers**.

- **Belt A:** 8 strakes
- **Belt B:** 8 strakes
- **Belt C:** 8 strakes
- **Belt D:** 7 strakes, including the garboard

### Procedure

1. Use a tick strip to transfer the belt divisions to the bulkheads.
2. Tack temporary battens to the marked points.
3. Begin checking the run around Bulkhead H.
4. Sight from bow, stern and side.
5. Adjust the battens until the curves are smooth and symmetrical.
6. Use the hull profile on Sheet 2 to check the aft run.

Do not begin permanent planking until the belt layout is fair.

## 3. Planking Butts

The manual's butt pattern is deliberate and staggered.

1. Plan the butt sequence before cutting planks.
2. Keep butts on bulkhead locations where the manual calls for them.
3. Stagger adjacent strakes.
4. Avoid a ladder pattern.
5. Record any altered butt location in your build log.

### Belt A

Follow the manual's specified H/L/D/G staggered sequence rather than inventing a generic butt pattern.

### Belts B–D

Continue the staggered pattern shown in the manual and Sheet 2.

## 4. Spiling

1. Make a paper template.
2. Transfer the hull curve.
3. Transfer the template to plank stock.
4. Cut slightly oversize.
5. Sand to fit.
6. Dry-fit the full plank.
7. Check neighboring plank transitions.
8. Duplicate the successful plank where appropriate.

## 5. Fastening the Planks

Practice the fastening method on scrap first.

- [ ] Adhesive amount controlled.
- [ ] Temporary pins/clamps ready.
- [ ] Butt joints supported.
- [ ] Squeeze-out can be cleaned immediately.

## 6. Outer Hull

### General workflow

For every plank:

1. Identify its belt and strake.
2. Determine taper from the plan.
3. Bend/shape the plank.
4. Dry-fit the complete length.
5. Check every bulkhead contact.
6. Check bow and stern landings.
7. Check butt location.
8. Apply adhesive.
9. Secure without forcing.
10. Clean squeeze-out.
11. Allow to cure.
12. Inspect before the next strake.

### Bow

If the plank buckles, twists, or springs away:

- correct taper;
- improve the bend;
- re-spile if necessary.

Do not force a bad fit with glue.

### Stern

The aft hull requires significant taper. Use the Sheet 2 profile to judge the aft plank widths and run. Do not create random wedges simply to close a gap.

### Belt D / garboard

The garboard and aft Belt D planks require special attention to the Sheet 2 profile because the complete aft arrangement is not represented by the basic belt drawing alone.

### Fairness gate

- [ ] No hard steps between strakes.
- [ ] No hollowed bulkhead areas.
- [ ] Bow runs fair.
- [ ] Stern runs fair.
- [ ] Butt pattern consistent.

## 7. Planking Inboard — Ceiling Planks

The manual specifies ceiling planking for the **inboard transom**, using 3/64" planks.

1. Prepare the transom surface.
2. Cut planks to fit.
3. Dry-fit.
4. Install.
5. Clean adhesive.
6. Sand only as needed.

Do not treat the entire interior as a conventional ceiling-planked hull.

## 8. Planking the Decks

**Deck planking belongs to Stage 2.**

### Prepare coamings before deck planking

The manual's recommended approach is to install the coamings and then plank around them.

1. Locate hatch, cabin, skylight and companionway coamings.
2. Build/fit the coamings.
3. Glue them to their appropriate bulkheads/deck structure.
4. Add **1/8" scrap support under free coaming sides** so deck planks have a permanent landing.
5. Check the support will not interfere with the visible deck.

### Deck-planking sequence

1. Establish the centerline.
2. Study the plan's butt pattern.
3. Mark openings.
4. Install the reference plank.
5. Work outward symmetrically.
6. Maintain plank width.
7. Stagger butts.
8. Fit carefully around coamings.
9. Shape the perimeter.
10. Treat the waterway edge as shown on the plan.

### Nibbing

A nibbing strake is the preferred shipwright treatment at the waterway, but the manual also permits feathering the deck planks instead.

Choose one method and use it consistently.
